Reading the numbers

Every column is computed in the page from the raw win and loss counts.

EV — expected points per trade

EV = WinRate × TP − (1 − WinRate) × SL

Multiply by $2 for dollars per contract. This is the only column that answers whether a setup makes money — a high win rate on its own does not.

EV low — the conservative number

A 96% win rate from 5 losses in 159 trades and a 96% win rate from 40 losses in 1000 trades are not the same claim. This column takes the pessimistic end of what the sample supports and recomputes EV from it.

Rank by this, not by EV. It penalises small samples automatically.

Rows that aren't real results

On 1-minute bars, when a single bar contains both the stop and the target, the backtest has to guess which was hit first — and it guesses in your favour.

Any configuration with a stop of 20 points or less is inside ordinary bar noise on MNQ, so its win rate measures that guess rather than the market. Those rows are hidden by default; the toggle above the table brings them back if you want to see what was excluded.

Drawdown — the missing number

EV tells you where the equity curve ends, not how ugly the path is. With fixed stops and targets the path is a random walk of known step sizes, so an estimate can be derived from win rate, SL, TP and trade count. Switch the table to full detail for Est. max DD and Ret/DD.

It reorders things sharply. Two configs near 11–12 EV can differ by six times in expected drawdown, because a 62% win rate on a 120-point stop strings losses together in a way a 94% win rate never does.

Treat it as a floor, not a forecast. It assumes trades are independent; real losses cluster by regime, so live drawdown runs worse. A proper equity curve from the backtest is still the thing to build.

NY PM has no edge

Every NY PM configuration with a realistic stop lost money. The search collapsed almost the whole PM grid to a 10-point stop — what happens when there is nothing worth risking.

Five PM short rows sit at 75–77% win rate and carry EV between −4.65 and −6.26 points. The clearest possible proof that win rate is not an edge.

Edge — margin above break-even

Break-even = SL ÷ (SL + TP)

Almost everything here risks more than it targets, so the win rate carries the whole edge. Edge is how many points of win rate you can lose before EV reaches zero.

EV/Risk — capital efficiency

EV ÷ SL

A 12-point EV on a 105-point stop and a 9-point EV on a 25-point stop are not comparable until you normalise. At equal dollar risk you trade four times the size on the tighter stop.

Backtested in Python on 1-minute MNQ data, first touch only, one trade per level per session. No commission or slippage applied; MNQ round-trip runs roughly $1 per contract. Trade counts fall as the offset widens and as the stop widens within the same entry, which suggests unresolved trades are dropped at the session boundary rather than force-closed — if so, wide-stop rows are flattered, because the dropped trades are the slow indecisive ones rather than a random sample. Historical behaviour, not a prediction.
